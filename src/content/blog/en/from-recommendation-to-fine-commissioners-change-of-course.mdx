Anyone who followed the practice of the Commissioner for the Right to Information and Personal Data Protection over the past decade remembers the pattern well: administrative investigation, findings of violations, a recommendation with deadlines — and there the story ended. A fine was a rare, almost symbolic exception. Businesses understood the game, and recommendations ended up in drawers.

That era is over. The Commissioner's latest decision-making — read in sequence, from the recommendations of past years to the fines of **30.01.2026** and back to the recommendations of **17.04.2026** — shows an institution that has not simply changed its tone, but has built a new enforcement architecture. In our assessment, this is the most important development in Albanian data protection practice since Law 124/2024 entered into force.

## The era of recommendations: why it seemed "nothing ever happened"

Under the previous law no. 9887/2008, the Commissioner's main instrument was the recommendation. Controllers were found in breach — missing privacy notices, undefined retention periods, weak security — and received a list of tasks with deadlines. Sanctions existed on paper but were applied rarely, and in amounts with no deterrent effect.

That model had its own logic: the market was unprepared, data protection culture was embryonic, and an authority that opened with massive fines would have produced more resistance than compliance. But it also carried an obvious cost — the recommendation without consequences taught the market that non-compliance was risk-free.

## The infrastructure of the turn: 2024-2025

The change of course did not arrive as a sudden decision. It was built patiently, stone by stone, over two years:

**Law 124/2024** introduced sanction ceilings of a different order — up to **924,000 Lekë or 4% of annual turnover** for violations of principles, legal bases and data subject rights (Article 94), following the GDPR model.

**Instruction no. 06 of 16.07.2025** approved the fine calculation methodology — a transparent algorithm weighing gravity, duration, intent or negligence, and the degree of cooperation. Seemingly a technical detail, in substance a strategic precondition: a fine calculated under a published methodology is far easier to defend before the Administrative Court than a number set ad hoc.

The **investigation orders of 02.09.2025** opened systematic inquiries into the IT sector, focused on technical and organisational measures and the information security management system (ISMS).

As this sequence shows, the Commissioner first built the legal basis, then the methodology, then the investigations — and only then struck. That is not the behaviour of an impulsive institution; it is the behaviour of an institution preparing the ground for sanctions that will hold up on the merits.

## 30.01.2026: the day everything changed

With Decisions no. 02, 03 and 04, the Commissioner fined three IT-sector companies on the same day — **Engineering Albania**, **Instant AL** and **Sisal Albania**. The fines were cumulative (for Instant AL: **462,000 Lekë** for violating Articles 6 and 13, plus **231,000 Lekë** for violating Article 28) and came with corrective obligations on strict deadlines: 15, 30, 45 and 60 days.

We analysed those decisions in detail in our article on the [five lessons from the January decisions](/en/blog/commissioner-started-fines-5-lessons). Here a different question matters: why were these companies fined, when hundreds of others with the same shortcomings had received only recommendations over the years?

The answer lies in the reasoning of the decisions themselves. The sanctioned controllers had presented formal policies never implemented, trainings without any documentary evidence, modules based solely on the GDPR rather than Albanian legislation, and arguments of the "we are revising our policies" kind — which the Commissioner rejected with what is now a familiar formula: a draft is not compliance. After years of public warnings, instructions and sector recommendations, the Commissioner's position was clear: the time of open-ended tolerance was over.

## 17.04.2026: back to recommendations? No — their evolution

Three months after the fines, the Commissioner issued four simultaneous Recommendations (no. 20-23) to construction-sector companies: **Kron Construction**, **Klar**, **Lim-Em** and **Kontakt**. No fines — a formal warning under Article 83/2/a.

A careless reader might take this as a softening. In our assessment the opposite is true — and this is the key to understanding the Commissioner's current decision-making.

**First**, the 2026 recommendation is not the 2019 recommendation. The Commissioner states it expressly in the text: in case of non-implementation within the deadlines, it will proceed under Articles 92, 93 and 94 — that is, a fine, calculated under the Instruction 06/2025 methodology. Today's recommendation is a documented last chance, not the closing of a file.

**Second**, the difference in treatment between January and April was not accidental. All four construction companies cooperated intensively during the investigation — they submitted ISMS documentation, and some appointed a DPO during the proceedings themselves. Cooperation during the investigation transformed what could have been a fining decision into a recommendation without a fine. That is a clear strategic message to every controller: your conduct during the investigation determines the kind of act you receive at the end.

**Third**, the April findings went considerably deeper than January's: biometric entry-exit data without specific rules, shared networks between same-group companies without segmentation, no domain controller, security appliances without IPS/IDS, cameras covering the public street, candidates' CVs kept indefinitely after recruitment, confidentiality declarations still citing the repealed law 9887/2008. The Commissioner is no longer checking merely "whether you have a regulation" — it is checking the technical architecture of the processing.

## The logic of the new course: three readings

**The institutional reading.** Law 124/2024 binds the Commissioner to the accountability principle and places it under the scrutiny of the EU integration process. An authority that only recommends does not pass the equivalence test with its EU counterparts; an authority that fines under a transparent methodology does.

**The strategic reading.** The campaigns are sectoral: IT in January, construction in April. This is not chance but method — each campaign produces documented precedents for its sector, and the mere publication of the decisions does the preventive work for the rest of the market. The only open question is which sector comes next: private healthcare, education, tourism and HoReCa are the obvious candidates.

**The practical reading.** The combination of "harsh fines for the uncooperative + a recommendation as a last chance for the cooperative" is precisely the model of established European authorities. The Commissioner has not simply changed course — it has adopted an incentive system in which proactive compliance and cooperation are rewarded, while passivity is priced.

## What you should do today

Under these conditions, our advice to businesses is direct:

**If you have received nothing from the Commissioner** — do not read the silence as safety. The internal audit should happen now, not when the investigation order arrives: a record of processing activities (RoPA), a specific internal regulation under Law 124/2024, a published privacy policy, a DPA with every processor, a DPO where required, trainings with documentary evidence and — for large entities — an ISO 27001-based ISMS.

**If you have received a recommendation** — treat it as a preliminary fine under a suspensive condition. The 15/30/45/60-day deadlines are real, and the final notification to the Commissioner must be accompanied by evidence for every point of the operative part, not by promises.

**If you are under investigation** — cooperate without reservation. The April 2026 practice proves it with facts: cooperation during the investigation is the difference between a recommendation without a fine and a decision with one.

In light of the above, the Commissioner's latest decision-making reads clearly only as a whole: yesterday's recommendations built the file, January's fines set the precedent, April's recommendations defined the new rules of the game. Those who understand this sequence still have time to act. Those who wait for the next proof are, in all likelihood, going to be part of it.
