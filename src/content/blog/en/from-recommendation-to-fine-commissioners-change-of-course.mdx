On **12.06.2026**, the Office of the Commissioner for the Right to Information and Personal Data Protection published three sanctioning decisions worth approximately **1 million euros** in total — against construction companies **Agi Kons** and **Progeen**, and the pharmacy chain **Ditë e Natë**. They are the highest sanctions ever imposed since Law 124/2024 entered into force.

The figure draws attention, but it is not the real news. The real news is that the violations fined in June are, overwhelmingly, the same violations for which the Commissioner — only two months earlier — had issued mere recommendations. Same conduct, different instrument. In our assessment, this is the most significant change of course in Albanian data protection practice, and it deserves to be read across its full sequence.

## The era of recommendations: why it seemed "nothing ever happened"

Under the previous law no. 9887/2008, the Commissioner's near-exclusive instrument was the recommendation. Controllers were found in breach — missing privacy notices, undefined retention periods, weak security — and received a list of tasks with deadlines. Sanctions existed on paper but were rarely applied, and in amounts with no deterrent effect. That model had its own logic in an unprepared market, but also an obvious cost: the recommendation without consequences taught the market that non-compliance was risk-free.

## The infrastructure of the turn: 2024-2025

The change did not arrive as a sudden decision — it was built stone by stone over two years.

**Law 124/2024** introduced sanction ceilings of a different order, following the GDPR model. **Instruction no. 06 of 16.07.2025** approved the fine calculation methodology — a transparent algorithm weighing gravity, duration, intent or negligence, and the degree of cooperation. Seemingly a technical detail, in substance a strategic precondition: a fine calculated under a published methodology is far easier to defend before the Administrative Court than an ad hoc figure. The **investigation orders of 02.09.2025** then opened the first systematic sector inquiries.

As this sequence shows, the Commissioner first built the legal basis, then the methodology, then the investigations — and only then struck.

## January-April 2026: the first two tests

On **30.01.2026** came the first real fines: Decisions no. 02-04 against three IT companies (Engineering Albania, Instant AL, Sisal Albania), worth around 95 thousand euros in total. We analysed them in our article on the [five lessons from the January decisions](/en/blog/commissioner-started-fines-5-lessons). The signal was clear, but many read it as an isolated episode.

On **17.04.2026** the Commissioner issued four simultaneous Recommendations (no. 20-23) to construction companies — Kron Construction, Klar, Lim-Em, Kontakt. No fines — a warning under Article 83/2/a, but with a sentence that then looked like boilerplate and today reads as official notice: in case of non-implementation, the Commissioner proceeds under Articles 92, 93 and 94. The findings were more detailed than ever: employee photos and videos on social media without consent, cameras covering the public street, undefined retention periods, missing DPAs with processors, no DPO, security measures below standard.

Those four companies escaped without fines for one documented reason: they cooperated intensively during the investigation — submitting security documentation, some appointing a DPO during the proceedings themselves. The April recommendation was the sector's last chance, put in writing.

## 12.06.2026: the real turn

Two months later, the same institution, facing the same violations, chose the other instrument.

**Agi Kons** (construction) was fined **24 million Lekë** for violating Articles 6, 7 and 8 — the basic processing principles and the conditions of consent — after the investigation found photographs and videos of employees and other individuals published on Instagram without proof of consent. On top came a fine of **11.11 million Lekë** for gaps in process documentation, security measures, and the unformalised relationship with the company contracted to maintain its website — lacking the technical and organisational obligations the law requires when processing is delegated. Total: **35.11 million Lekë**, around **360 thousand euros**.

**Progeen** (construction) was fined **3 million Lekë** for violating Article 13 — the duty to inform data subjects — and **18 million Lekë** for violations of Articles 27, 28 and 33: the record of processing activities, security of processing, and the data protection officer. Total: **21 million Lekë**, around **220 thousand euros**.

**Ditë e Natë** (pharmacy chain) was sanctioned for violations of Articles 6, 7, 8 and 13: indefinite retention of customer data, a CCTV system that also filmed public spaces, Instagram publications without proof of consent, incomplete privacy notices, no deletion procedures, and insufficient security measures.

Now compare June's list with April's: social media publications without consent, CCTV over public spaces, undefined retention, missing DPAs, no DPO. It is the same list. What was recommendation material in April became fine material in June — and not just any fine: from roughly 95 thousand euros for January's three companies to roughly 1 million euros for June's three. An order-of-magnitude jump within five months.

## Why the course changed: three readings

**The institutional reading.** The Commissioner has articulated it itself: the June decisions mark the transition from the education and orientation phase to the effective application of the law's punitive mechanisms. An authority that only recommends does not pass the equivalence test with its European counterparts — and Albania is in an integration process whose chapters demand precisely demonstrable enforcement.

**The strategic reading.** The campaigns are sectoral and escalating: IT in January with moderate fines, construction in April with warning-recommendations, construction again in June with heavy fines, plus the extension to pharmaceuticals. Each campaign produces documented precedents for its sector, and the mere publication of the decisions does the preventive work for the rest of the market. Sectors with mass public contact — private healthcare, education, tourism, retail — have every reason to consider themselves next.

**The practical reading.** The combination of "a recommendation as a last chance for the cooperative + harsh fines for the rest" is precisely the model of established European authorities. The Commissioner has not simply changed its tone — it has built an incentive system in which proactive compliance and cooperation during the investigation are rewarded, while passivity is now priced in tens of millions of Lekë.

## What you should do today

Under these conditions, our advice to businesses is direct:

**If you have received nothing from the Commissioner** — do not read the silence as safety. Check today the points that were fined in June: do you publish employee photos and videos on social media without a consent form? Do your cameras film the street? Do you have documented retention periods, a record of processing activities, an internal regulation under Law 124/2024, a DPA with every processor — including whoever maintains your website — and a DPO where required? Each of these points now has a published price.

**If you have received a recommendation** — treat it as a preliminary fine under a suspensive condition. June's practice proved it: the deadlines are real, and the final notification to the Commissioner must be accompanied by evidence for every point of the operative part, not by promises.

**If you are under investigation** — cooperate without reservation. The difference between April and June shows it in figures: cooperation during the investigation is the difference between a recommendation without a fine and a decision worth tens of millions of Lekë.

In light of the above, the Commissioner's latest decision-making reads clearly only as a whole: the recommendations of past years prepared the ground, January's fines set the precedent, April's recommendations were the last documented warning, and the decisions of 12 June definitively closed the tolerance phase. Those who understand this sequence still have time to act. Those who wait for the next proof are, in all likelihood, going to be part of it.
